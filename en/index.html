The science behind each ingredient

We don't cite studies on "Crave Off" — we cite the real, published research on what's inside it.

🌿 Gymnema sylvestre — the sugar-craving killer

In a published human trial, people who took Gymnema reported a measurable drop in their desire for high-sugar sweet foods — it literally blunts the brain's reward from sweetness. This is the closest thing to a "craving off switch" in the botanical world.

Turner et al., Physiology & Behavior, 2020 — randomized human trial on sugar cravings.

🍵 Green tea catechins (EGCG) — the fat-metabolism nudge

A meta-analysis of randomized trials found green tea catechins produced a small but statistically significant reduction in body weight and helped maintain weight after loss — working through fat oxidation, not stimulation.

Hursel et al., Int. Journal of Obesity, 2009 — meta-analysis of 11 studies.

🫐 Wild Amazonian açaí — the clean-energy engine

Açaí's anthocyanins are some of the most antioxidant-dense compounds in nature. In human exercise trials, an açaí beverage increased time-to-exhaustion and improved antioxidant capacity — that's the "steady energy, no crash" most women feel first.

Carvalho-Peixoto et al., Applied Physiology, Nutrition & Metabolism, 2015.

🍋 Garcinia (HCA) — the appetite assist

Garcinia's hydroxycitric acid has been studied for its role in appetite support and curbing the drive to overeat — a classic botanical in the craving-control stack.
